## 1. Paste-ready text: "Support from the scientific literature"

More than 1 billion people worldwide live with a mental disorder, with anxiety and depressive
disorders the most common and incidence peaking between ages 15 and 19 (World Health
Organization, 2025; GBD 2023 Mental Disorders Collaborators, 2026). Access has not kept pace:
137 million Americans live in federally designated mental health professional shortage areas
where only 27% of need is met (KFF, 2026). Among U.S. college students, 37% screen positive for
moderate-to-severe depression, 32% for moderate-to-severe anxiety, and 11% report past-year
suicidal ideation; roughly 40% of symptomatic students receive no treatment, most often citing
time, cost, or a preference to manage on their own (Healthy Minds Network, 2025). Counseling
centers report anxiety as the leading presenting concern and record-high proportions of clients
with prior treatment and prior suicide attempts (Center for Collegiate Mental Health, 2025).

Into this gap, students are already using unsupervised general-purpose AI. Half of U.S. adults
now use AI chatbots and one in ten use them for emotional support (Pew Research Center, 2026).
Among adults 18 to 49, 35% use AI for mental health support at least weekly, and those with
moderate-to-severe symptoms or suicidal ideation are the most likely to be heavy users (Ueda et
al., 2026). Use of generative AI for mental health advice among Americans aged 12 to 21 rose
from 13% to 19% during 2025, and 63% of those users had told no one (McBain, Bozick, et al.,
2025; McBain, Cantor, et al., 2026). OpenAI itself estimates that roughly 0.15% of weekly users,
over a million people, have conversations containing explicit indicators of suicidal planning
(OpenAI, 2025). Leading commentators conclude that people are using large language models as de
facto therapists and that the field needs independently run trials rather than more speculation
(Rousmaniere, Goldberg, & Torous, 2026; Torous & Topol, 2025).

The efficacy evidence for generative AI agents matured substantially in the past year. The first
randomized trial of a generative AI therapy chatbot (Therabot; N = 210) found large
between-group reductions in depression (d = 0.85 to 0.90), anxiety (d = 0.79 to 0.84), and
eating-disorder risk over four to eight weeks, with working alliance ratings comparable to
outpatient psychotherapy; notably, human monitors intervened 28 times, and the authors conclude
that no generative agent is ready to operate unsupervised (Heinz et al., 2025). A three-arm
trial with 995 university students found a conversational AI agent used over 12 weeks reduced
anxiety more than both waitlist and face-to-face group therapy, reduced depression more than
waitlist, and showed that digital therapeutic alliance predicted both engagement and symptom
change (Shoshani et al., 2026). Smaller trials and feasibility studies in young adults and
college students report consistent short-term symptom reductions, high acceptability, and heavy
off-hours use as an adjunct to human care (Zhao et al., 2025; Reyes-Portillo et al., 2025;
Pinna et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026). Three 2025 to 2026 meta-analyses converge on
small-to-moderate pooled effects for chatbot interventions on depression and anxiety (g or SMD
of about 0.2 to 0.3), larger in clinical and subclinical samples, with too few generative-AI
trials to establish whether they outperform rule-based designs (Sohn et al., 2026; Mokhtari
Masoumi Alamdarloo et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2025). A systematic review of 160 studies found
that only 16% of LLM-based chatbot studies had reached clinical efficacy testing (Hua et al.,
2025), and a consensus statement on digital mental health engagement calls for pre-specified
usage metrics and longitudinal designs that can relate dose to outcome (Smith et al., 2025).

The safety literature matured in parallel and now defines what responsible deployment requires.
Audits show that unmodified frontier models express stigma toward some conditions, respond
inappropriately to delusions and to intermediate-risk suicide queries, and sometimes answer
lethality-of-means questions (Moore et al., 2025; McBain, Cantor, Zhang, et al., 2025), and that
consumer companion chatbots recognize the need for escalation in only 40% of simulated
adolescent crises (Brewster et al., 2025). Clinical case series and a psychiatric service
record screen document delusion reinforcement among vulnerable users (Olsen et al., 2026;
Morrin et al., 2026), and a 119-study scoping review catalogs sycophancy, hallucination,
overreliance, and dependent use as the principal harm categories while noting that
longitudinal outcome data remain scarce (Diel et al., 2026). In response, the American
Psychological Association (2025) issued a health advisory calling for randomized and
longitudinal studies with crisis safeguards and human oversight; the U.S. Food and Drug
Administration's Digital Health Advisory Committee (2025) identified missed crisis detection,
sycophancy, parasocial attachment, and model drift as the core hazards and emphasized
human-in-the-loop escalation and lifecycle monitoring; and Utah's mental health chatbot law
(H.B. 452, effective May 2025) sets expectations for licensed clinician involvement,
pre-deployment and ongoing safety testing, real-time response to acute risk, and prioritizing
user safety over engagement. Reviews of implementation barriers identify inadequate crisis
detection as the most cited obstacle (Shankar, Lim, & Xu, 2026), and current expert consensus
holds that the most defensible model is human-in-the-loop, stepped, and bounded by explicit
handoff rules (Paulus, 2026). Licensed therapists likewise report trusting generative AI in
clinician-supervised, between-session roles while rejecting autonomous high-stakes judgment
(Kuang, Pope, & Zhang, 2026; Hipgrave et al., 2025), and a new taxonomy places
therapist-in-the-loop systems as a distinct design tier requiring its own efficacy evidence
(Schuster et al., 2026).

Phase 1 of this program (BYU IRB 2025-519) established that single, supervised sessions with
our voice-enabled AI support agent are feasible, safe, and perceived as helpful by student
participants, and gathered licensed therapists' perspectives on appropriate roles for such
tools. Three gaps remain that only a longitudinal design can address. First, working alliance
with a conversational agent accrues over repeated use and shows modest but measurable
associations with outcomes (Malouin-Lachance et al., 2025; Schlapfer et al., 2026; Xu et al.,
2025), so it cannot be characterized from a single session. Second, the safety literature now
argues that single-turn tests miss multi-week drift such as compulsive use, sleep loss, and
withdrawal, and that trajectory-level monitoring is required (Morrin, Au Yeung, et al., 2026);
attrition in AI-based mental health interventions is meaningfully lower when safety measures
are present (Yang, Liu, et al., 2026). Third, voice remains essentially unstudied: the one
large randomized comparison found no main effect of voice versus text on psychosocial outcomes
but a dose-dependent pattern in which brief daily voice use appeared benign and prolonged use
did not (Fang et al., 2025, preprint), speech carries depression-relevant prosodic signal not
present in text (Gomez-Zaragoza et al., 2026), and clinicians have raised voice-specific
concerns that warrant monitoring (Augustin & Ostergaard, 2026). No published trial combines a
voice-based generative agent, structured human oversight, and a formal crisis-detection
pipeline in a U.S. undergraduate population over multiple weeks. Phase 2 is designed to fill
that gap while meeting or exceeding the safeguards that the professional, regulatory, and
statutory literature now expects.

## 2. Annotated bibliography by theme

### 2.1 Efficacy and outcome trials of generative agents

- **Heinz et al. 2025, NEJM AI (Therabot RCT).** N = 210, 4-week active + 4-week follow-up, adults with MDD/GAD/CHR-FED recruited nationally. MDD d = 0.85 (4 wk) / 0.90 (8 wk); GAD d = 0.79 / 0.84; CHR-FED d = 0.63 / 0.82. WAI-SR mean 3.59, described as comparable to outpatient benchmarks. 95% used the app, mean 6.18 h. **28 staff interventions** (15 suicidal ideation, 13 inappropriate chatbot output). The strongest single citation for both efficacy and the need for human oversight.
- **Shoshani et al. 2026, JAMA Netw Open.** 995 Israeli university students, 12 weeks, AI agent vs face-to-face group therapy vs waitlist. AI beat both on GAD-7 (MD about -2.2), beat waitlist on PHQ-9 (MD -2.0), beat both on WHO-5. Alliance predicted engagement (beta 0.31) and symptom change (beta -0.58). Closest analogue to Phase 2 (students, multi-week, PHQ/GAD, alliance as mediator).
- **Zhao et al. 2025, Appl Psychol Health Well-Being.** 865 young adults, 28-day waitlist RCT of daily LLM dialogue; depression reduced at 2 wk, depression and anxiety at 4 wk. Effect sizes not in abstract.
- **Kuta et al. 2026, JMIR Ment Health.** 185 adults, 3 weeks: solution-focused LLM bot vs plain ChatGPT vs control. PHQ-9 d = -0.47 vs -0.44; GAD-7 ns. **52% dropout**, 66% in the structured arm. Evidence that general LLMs perform about as well as purpose-built ones short-term, and a warning about unsupervised attrition.
- **Yang, Wu, et al. 2026, Appl Psychol Health Well-Being.** 99 college students, 14 days, emotional-support vs problem-solving bot vs waitlist; both reduced anxiety, not depression; agent function shaped which outcomes moved.
- **Reyes-Portillo et al. 2025, JMIR Form Res.** 50 diverse US college students, 1-week open trial (Wayhaven); significant within-person reductions in depression, anxiety, hopelessness; well-being d = 0.45.
- **Pinna et al. 2026, JMIR Form Res.** 42 university students, 2-4 weeks (Safespace); use peaked early morning and late night; higher-symptom students disclosed emotionally in 99% of sessions; valued as blended-care tool between counseling sessions. Directly supports own-device, off-hours adjunct model and flags safety-pipeline load from higher-symptom users.
- **Cachia et al. 2025 (arXiv 2601.11530, preprint).** 486 undergraduates, 3 US universities, 6-week waitlist RCT of a GenAI well-being app; gains in positive affect, resilience, belonging. Cite only as preprint.
- **Van Swearingen et al. 2026 (arXiv 2606.28241, preprint, industry).** 1,284 Ash users, 4 weeks; small within-person gains (d 0.14-0.26); active days and minutes, not message counts, predicted outcomes. Supports time-based engagement metrics.
- **Callahan et al. 2026, JMIR Form Res (Headspace Ebb).** 482 surveyed, 393,969 users' engagement data; users want an adjunct to human care; adding memory and safety detection raised 7-day retention from 28.5% to 50.8%.

### 2.2 Meta-analyses and reviews

- **Sohn et al. 2026, npj Digit Med.** 38 RCTs (n = 7,401) depression g = 0.31; 34 RCTs anxiety g = 0.28; effect scales with severity (clinical 0.64, subclinical 0.34, nonclinical 0.07). Generative subgroup (8 trials) underpowered. 35/39 high risk of bias.
- **Mokhtari Masoumi Alamdarloo et al. 2026, npj Digit Med.** 48 RCTs, 28,071 participants; SMD -0.27 depression, -0.20 anxiety, -0.26 stress; no architecture subgroup differences (2 generative trials).
- **Zhang et al. 2025, JMIR.** First GenAI-only meta-analysis: 14 RCTs (N = 6,314), ES = 0.30 [0.004, 0.59]. Barely significant, wide CI.
- **Hua et al. 2025, World Psychiatry.** 160 studies 2020-2024; LLM bots 45% of new studies in 2024 but only 16% reached efficacy testing; three-tier evaluation framework.
- **Yang, Liu, et al. 2026, BMC Med Ethics.** 39 studies; pooled attrition 17%; generative 9% vs retrieval-based 23%; safety measures moderate attrition. Updates the Torous 2020 attrition citation.
- **Smith et al. 2025, npj Digit Med.** 20-author consensus on engagement and attrition; recommends standardized engagement metrics and reporting of harms.
- **Im & Woo 2025, JMIR Ment Health.** Narrative review of CBT-chatbot mechanisms (restructuring, behavioral activation, mindfulness).
- **Shankar, Lim, & Xu 2026, Healthcare.** 27 studies, CFIR-mapped; inadequate crisis detection is the most cited barrier (21/27); 24/7 availability the top facilitator.
- **Diel et al. 2026, npj Digit Med.** 119-study scoping review of LLM chatbot harms; longitudinal outcome data scarce.

### 2.3 Working alliance with AI agents

- **Malouin-Lachance et al. 2025, JMIR Ment Health.** Integrative review (28 studies); DTA framework of goals, tasks, bond.
- **Xu et al. 2025, JMIR Ment Health.** 4-week crossover diary study (Woebot, Wysa), 26 mostly student participants; 18/26 reported a bond; themes on empathy expectations and privacy.
- **Schlapfer et al. 2026, Internet Interv.** WAI-I in a conversational-agent RCT (N = 117); alliance to week-10 distress partial r = -0.18; task and goal subscales matter more than bond. Validates a WAI adaptation and tempers expected effect sizes.
- **Naeim & Narimani 2026, Psychiatry Res.** 34 professionals in 8 countries; introduces "hybrid therapeutic alliance" construct.

### 2.4 Safety, harms, and crisis handling

- **Moore et al. 2025, FAccT.** Stigma toward alcohol dependence and schizophrenia; therapists appropriate 93% vs LLMs under 80%; delusions about 45% appropriate; canonical "bridges taller than 25 m" failure. Bigger models did not fix it.
- **McBain, Cantor, Zhang, et al. 2025, Psychiatr Serv.** 9,000 responses; chatbots aligned with clinicians only at extreme risk levels; sometimes answered lethality-of-means questions. Justifies a graded classifier with escalation thresholds.
- **Brewster et al. 2025, JAMA Netw Open.** 25 chatbots, 75 simulated adolescent crises; 46.7% appropriate overall; companion bots recognized escalation need 40% vs 90% for general assistants.
- **Arnaiz-Rodriguez et al. 2026, JMIR Ment Health.** Six-category crisis taxonomy, 2,000+ input eval set, appropriateness protocol. Reusable for validating classifier categories.
- **Bentley et al. 2026, JMIR AI (VERA-MH).** Clinician-validated open suicide-safety benchmark; LLM-judge vs clinician IRR 0.81. Candidate standard for pre-deployment and periodic re-testing.
- **Schoene et al. 2026 (arXiv 2606.23884, preprint).** Safeguards consistent only for suicide/self-harm; near-total failure for eating disorders, substance use, MDD under adversarial prompting.
- **Au Yeung et al. 2025 (arXiv 2509.10970, preprint, psychosis-bench).** Delusion confirmation 0.91, harm enablement 0.69; safety interventions in only 37% of applicable turns; worse with implicit cues.
- **Ostergaard 2025, Acta Psychiatr Scand** (two editorials); **Olsen et al. 2026** (38 EHR-identified patients with chatbot-related harm, mostly delusion consolidation); **Morrin et al. 2026, Lancet Psychiatry** (mechanisms and safeguards: instruction protocols, reflective check-ins, escalation); **Augustin, Pollak, & Morrin 2026, NPP Digit Psychiatry** ("amplification spiral"); **Morrin, Au Yeung, et al. 2026, JMIR Ment Health** (trajectories not endpoints); **Olisaeloka et al. 2026, BJPsych Open** (vulnerability x engagement x AI-characteristics model).
- **Chung, Bernier, & Hudon 2026, JMIR Ment Health.** 71 news articles, 36 cases; explicitly media-sourced, not clinically verified.
- **Chambers 2026, JAAPL.** Forensic editorial on Garcia v. Character Technologies and the 2025 state AG complaints.
- **Augustin & Ostergaard 2026, Acta Neuropsychiatr.** Editorial on voice-specific risks (persuasiveness, fewer reflective pauses).

### 2.5 Hybrid care and clinician attitudes

- **Rousmaniere, Goldberg, & Torous 2026, Lancet Psychiatry**; **Torous & Topol 2025, Lancet**; **Paulus 2026, J Mood Anxiety Disord** (human-in-the-loop, stepped, bounded by handoff rules); **Schuster et al. 2026, JMIR Ment Health** (5-stage taxonomy; level 3 blended under oversight, level 4 therapist-in-the-loop).
- **Kuang, Pope, & Zhang 2026, JMIR** (18 US therapists; "hand on the wheel"); **Hipgrave et al. 2025, Front Digit Health** and **Goldie et al. 2025, JMIR Hum Factors** (23 Australian clinicians); **Johnson et al. 2026, JMIR** (278 Australian clinicians; 33% daily client-facing AI use).
- **Gratzer et al. 2026, Can J Psychiatry** (clinician's corner; ahead of print).

### 2.6 Voice modality

- **Fang et al. 2025 (arXiv 2503.17473, OpenAI/MIT, preprint).** 981 participants, 4 weeks, 3x3 text/neutral voice/engaging voice x task. No main effect of modality; voice better at brief daily use, worse with prolonged daily use; heavier use predicted worse outcomes.
- **Ngo & Sano 2025 (medRxiv, preprint).** Local Llama 3.1 8B voice pipeline; latency acceptable; high-risk scenarios remain weak.
- **Gomez-Zaragoza et al. 2026, Artif Intell Med.** 101 participants; depression detection F1 0.57 speech vs 0.33 text vs 0.65 multimodal; prosody carries most of the signal.
- Gap: no peer-reviewed efficacy trial of a voice-based LLM agent exists as of September 2026.

## 3. What changed since the September 2025 review

1. **Efficacy moved from one landmark RCT to a small set of controlled trials**, including a 995-student 12-week trial where the AI agent matched or beat group therapy. Three meta-analyses converge on pooled effects of about 0.2 to 0.3, larger in clinical samples. Generative-vs-rule-based superiority is still unproven.
2. **Harms moved from anecdote to data**: service-level EHR screen, a Lancet Psychiatry mechanistic model, a 119-study harm review, and audits showing companion bots escalate crises only 40% of the time.
3. **Evaluation matured**: a clinician-validated open suicide-safety benchmark (VERA-MH), a peer-reviewed crisis taxonomy, and a strong argument that multi-week trajectories, not single turns, are the right unit of safety analysis.
4. **Governance crystallized**: APA advisory, FDA advisory committee and discussion paper, FTC inquiry, and roughly 15 state laws converging on crisis detection, referral, disclosure, and reporting. Utah's law is in force and directly relevant.
5. **Real-world unsupervised use became measurable**: half of US adults use chatbots, one in ten for emotional support; youth use for mental health advice rose from 13% to 19% in one year and most tell no one.
6. **Voice remains the open gap**: one large preprint RCT (no main effect, dose-dependent pattern), one technical preprint, one editorial. No efficacy trial of a voice LLM agent exists.
