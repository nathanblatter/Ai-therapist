# Caseworker tutorial narration (2026-09-04). Slides from tutorial-screenshots/.

1. [title-caseworker] Welcome to the research portal. This tour covers the caseworker role — day-to-day participant support. You work from the summary data tier: AI summaries, risk signals, screeners, and safety plans — never verbatim transcripts. Everything you'll see is a practice sandbox with synthetic clients.

2. [c01-triage] Your day starts on Triage: caseload size, who needs attention, open crisis flags, and open escalations, ranked by urgency. The chips tell you why someone surfaced — a crisis flag, a low check-in mood, overdue practice.

3. [c02-participant-profile-summary-tier] A client's profile shows the session timeline, check-ins, safety plans, and your care notes. The amber notice marks what you don't see: clinical briefs and unredacted entries are therapist-only. That's the design — your view is built for support coordination, and anything needing clinical eyes gets escalated. Note drafts autosave; signing makes a note permanent.

4. [c03-work-queue] The work queue is your task list: crisis flags, worsening screeners, unread-message alerts. Ack claims an item so the team knows you're on it; Resolve closes it with a short note. Keep the urgent-safety email setting on.

5. [c04-escalation-composer] And the most important workflow: escalating. When something needs clinical judgment — disclosed ideation, a worrying pattern, anything beyond support coordination — pick the client, set the urgency, describe what changed and what you need, and raise it. The therapist takes it from there. When in doubt, escalate — nobody is ever faulted for asking for clinical eyes too early. The written guide covers messages, crisis views, and your caseload page. And one last thing: this is heavy work — the 988 and CAPS resources on every screen are for staff, too.
