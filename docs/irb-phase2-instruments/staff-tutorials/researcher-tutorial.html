Research Portal — Researcher Guide

How to use the AI Support Agent admin portal as a researcher — the study-operations role with the widest access. Screenshots come from the staging environment with synthetic data. Sign in at the study admin URL with your researcher account; enable MFA from the shield icon in the header on your first login.

1. Your home: Session History

Every session in the study, searchable by session ID, name, or username, with date and message-count filters. View opens the full transcript and insights; researchers hold the full-content data tier, so you see verbatim (redacted) conversation text. The demo/test rows you see here are excluded from all research exports automatically.

2. Live Monitoring

Real-time view of active sessions with counts for active users, messages, and crisis sessions. During study hours this is the "is anything happening right now" screen; the Crisis Only toggle filters to flagged sessions. From a live session you can observe and, when warranted, use the sideband controls to inject a message or end a session.

3. Analytics

Four tabs — Usage, Performance, Cost, Tools — over any date range. Session activity, voice-vs-text share, average duration, model spend, and which therapeutic tools the agent actually invokes. This is descriptive operations data; the research dataset proper comes from Export (section 9).

4. Safety: Crisis Management and Adverse Events

Crisis Management aggregates every crisis event, intervention, and risk assessment, with tabs for the full histories. Flags show what triggered them (auto vs manual) and the risk factors involved.

Adverse Events is your IRB duty station: high-severity crisis flags and distress survey responses auto-draft AE reports here with review deadlines (the nav badge turns red when something is overdue). Review each draft, complete it, and sign off — this queue is the source for IRB reporting.

5. Users

The account directory. Researchers are the only role that can create accounts and change roles — this is where you add research assistants, therapists, and caseworkers. MFA status, memory setting, and risk-context sharing are all visible per user.

6. Study Ops

The protocol's control panel: enrollment against target, condition-arm balance with an imbalance alarm, sessions-per-participant against the expected count, and protocol deviations. Run scan checks for new deviations on demand; the same scan runs on a schedule. Demo data is excluded.

7. Qualtrics: Sync and Survey Data

All five study surveys (baseline, weekly, exit, week-12, withdrawal) sync on a schedule; Sync now forces a run. Watch Linkage health: "unlinked finished" responses are ones that could not be matched to a participant (usually a mistyped study ID) and need human attention. Survey Data (next nav item) shows the per-participant completion matrix and scored instruments.

8. Evals

The safety-evaluation harness: nightly simulated voice sessions run through the real pipeline, judged against gating assertions, with pass-rate trends and per-run cost. Also home to eval calibration (LLM judge vs human ratings) and drift alerts. If a nightly run fails, the drill-down shows exactly which assertion broke.

9. Export

The de-identified research dataset: pseudonymized CSVs plus a generated codebook, deterministic for a given as-of date, demo data excluded, no message content or pseudonym mapping ever included. Redacted transcripts are a separate opt-in artifact handled as identifiable data. The lower form does operational JSON/CSV exports.

10. Onboarding staff: Sandbox Invites

Mint one-time links that create an isolated, pre-seeded practice environment (6-9 synthetic clients) for a new therapist or caseworker. Sandbox data never reaches research exports, crisis paging, or email — it is the safe way for new staff to learn the portal before touching real participants. Their tutorial (see the therapist and caseworker guides) assumes they start here.

11. System Prompts

The instructions the AI runs on, in three tabs: Realtime (Voice) Sessions, Chat-Only Sessions, and Therapeutic Modality (the CBT-informed layer that condition arms toggle). The {{crisis_text}} variable inserts the currently configured crisis contact line wherever it appears, so crisis numbers are edited once in System Config, never hardcoded here. Show Preview renders the final assembled prompt; Save Changes applies to new sessions only.

Study rule: during Phase 2 the prompt is a frozen instrument. The verbatim text is on file with the IRB, so a mid-study edit is a protocol event — it changes the intervention under test. Treat this page as read-and-verify: check the prompt matches the IRB-filed version, and coordinate with the PI (and an IRB amendment if substantive) before any edit. Every save is versioned and attributable.

12. Knowledge Base

The corpus the AI can retrieve mid-conversation: psychoeducation articles, techniques, and worksheets, each item tagged by topic and source. Only approved content is ever used in live sessions — new items sit in Pending approval until a researcher reviews them, so the approval gate is your quality control over what the AI can cite. Each card shows its source and license, when it was last retrieved, and Edit/Unapprove/Delete actions.

Two support tabs: Test retrieval lets you type a probe query ("can't sleep, mind racing") and see exactly which items the AI would pull, ranked — use it after adding content to confirm it surfaces. Retrieval log shows what was actually retrieved in real sessions, which tells you what participants are asking about and which items pull dead weight. Like the prompt, the corpus is part of the intervention: keep it stable during the study, and log any additions.

13. Redaction Review

Every message is automatically de-identified (names, places, identifying details) and the original text is deleted after the retention window; the redacted version is what persists and what research exports may include. This page is the human check on that pipeline: it lists redacted messages, oldest-verified-last, and you spot-check that (a) no identifying detail slipped through and (b) the redaction didn't gut the clinical meaning. If something slipped through, edit the text right here — your correction replaces the stored redacted content. A periodic pass (for example weekly, and before any transcript export) is a protocol expectation, not busywork: this is the layer that makes the rest of the data pipeline safe.

14. Consent Versions

The versioned consent copy participants accept in-app, in two audiences: Research (IRB study) — shown while the deployment runs in research mode — and Clinical (care team). The active version is the newest one whose effective date has passed; each participant's acceptance is stored with a hash of the exact text they saw, so you can always prove which words a given participant agreed to. Preview expands the full text; the acceptance count shows uptake.

Publish new version is how consent copy changes ship (for example after an IRB-approved amendment): publish with an effective date, and participants see the new text from then on — old acceptances keep pointing at the text they actually accepted. Never edit consent copy anywhere else; this page is the audit trail the IRB will ask for. In-app copy must always match the IRB-approved consent document version.

15. System Config and Data Retention

System Config holds the study-level operational settings. The two that matter most: Crisis Contact Information — the hotline name, number, and text line that {{crisis_text}} injects into prompts and that participants see in crisis moments — and On-Call Crisis Paging, which SMS-pages the on-call phone whenever a session is flagged high severity (the dashboard toast only reaches someone with the dashboard open; the pager is the net that works at 2 AM). Verify the on-call number at every handoff. Changes here are study-level decisions — coordinate before touching them.

Data Retention runs the IRB data-minimization commitment: original (pre-redaction) message text is automatically deleted after the retention period — but only once redaction of that message is complete, so nothing is lost before its redacted copy exists. The status cards are your morning glance: Ready to wipe and Awaiting redaction are normal flow; With errors counts messages whose redaction failed and therefore cannot be wiped — a nonzero number that persists needs attention, because those originals are outliving the window. The retention period and daily wipe time are IRB-filed parameters; do not change them without a protocol conversation. Below the settings, the deletion audit log records every wipe.

16. Triage and Caseload: the care-team side

Two nav items connect you to the care-team side. Triage is the same attention-ranked view therapists work from — clients, who needs attention, open crisis flags, open escalations. For you it is an oversight surface: see what the care team sees, audit that flags and escalations are being worked, and pull care context when reviewing an adverse event. The counts are scoped to care assignments, so outside a care deployment (as in this staging shot) it sits at zero.

Caseload is where the researcher's people-management duty meets the care side: it assigns participants to therapists, and therapists only ever see their assigned clients. Create the therapist account in Users first, then make the assignment here. The care workflows themselves (working the queue, escalations, progress notes, participant messaging) belong to the therapist and caseworker roles — the companion guides cover them.
