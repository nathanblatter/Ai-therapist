Research Portal — Researcher Guide

How to use the AI Support Agent admin portal as a researcher — the study-operations role with the widest access. Screenshots come from the staging environment with synthetic data. Sign in at the study admin URL with your researcher account; enable MFA from the shield icon in the header on your first login.

1. Your home: Session History

Every session in the study, searchable by session ID, name, or username, with date and message-count filters. View opens the full transcript and insights; researchers hold the full-content data tier, so you see verbatim (redacted) conversation text. The demo/test rows you see here are excluded from all research exports automatically.

2. Live Monitoring

Real-time view of active sessions with counts for active users, messages, and crisis sessions. During study hours this is the "is anything happening right now" screen; the Crisis Only toggle filters to flagged sessions. From a live session you can observe and, when warranted, use the sideband controls to inject a message or end a session.

3. Analytics

Four tabs — Usage, Performance, Cost, Tools — over any date range. Session activity, voice-vs-text share, average duration, model spend, and which therapeutic tools the agent actually invokes. This is descriptive operations data; the research dataset proper comes from Export (section 9).

4. Safety: Crisis Management and Adverse Events

Crisis Management aggregates every crisis event, intervention, and risk assessment, with tabs for the full histories. Flags show what triggered them (auto vs manual) and the risk factors involved.

Adverse Events is your IRB duty station: high-severity crisis flags and distress survey responses auto-draft AE reports here with review deadlines (the nav badge turns red when something is overdue). Review each draft, complete it, and sign off — this queue is the source for IRB reporting.

5. Users

The account directory. Researchers are the only role that can create accounts and change roles — this is where you add research assistants, therapists, and caseworkers. MFA status, memory setting, and risk-context sharing are all visible per user.

6. Study Ops

The protocol's control panel: enrollment against target, condition-arm balance with an imbalance alarm, sessions-per-participant against the expected count, and protocol deviations. Run scan checks for new deviations on demand; the same scan runs on a schedule. Demo data is excluded.

7. Qualtrics: Sync and Survey Data

All five study surveys (baseline, weekly, exit, week-12, withdrawal) sync on a schedule; Sync now forces a run. Watch Linkage health: "unlinked finished" responses are ones that could not be matched to a participant (usually a mistyped study ID) and need human attention. Survey Data (next nav item) shows the per-participant completion matrix and scored instruments.

8. Evals

The safety-evaluation harness: nightly simulated voice sessions run through the real pipeline, judged against gating assertions, with pass-rate trends and per-run cost. Also home to eval calibration (LLM judge vs human ratings) and drift alerts. If a nightly run fails, the drill-down shows exactly which assertion broke.

9. Export

The de-identified research dataset: pseudonymized CSVs plus a generated codebook, deterministic for a given as-of date, demo data excluded, no message content or pseudonym mapping ever included. Redacted transcripts are a separate opt-in artifact handled as identifiable data. The lower form does operational JSON/CSV exports.

10. Onboarding staff: Sandbox Invites

Mint one-time links that create an isolated, pre-seeded practice environment (6-9 synthetic clients) for a new therapist or caseworker. Sandbox data never reaches research exports, crisis paging, or email — it is the safe way for new staff to learn the portal before touching real participants. Their tutorial (see the therapist and caseworker guides) assumes they start here.

11. Also in your nav

  • System Prompts / Knowledge Base — the agent's instructions and psychoeducation corpus (edits are versioned; prompt changes mid-study are protocol events).
  • Redaction Review — spot-check the PHI redaction pipeline's output.
  • Consent Versions — every consent document version with content hashes.
  • System Config / Data Retention — feature flags, crisis contact config, retention windows, and the deletion audit log. Changes here are study-level decisions; coordinate before touching them.
  • Triage / Caseload — read-only views into the care-team side.

Questions or something looks wrong? The Report-a-problem button files directly to the engineering queue. Version 2026-09-04.
