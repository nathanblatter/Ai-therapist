Phase 2 Weekly Check-in Survey (Qualtrics) — DRAFT for IRB review

Administered online once per week during Weeks 1-8 (approximately 7 minutes). Participants may skip any question. Usage frequency and modality (voice/text) are measured objectively from app telemetry rather than self-report. Participants enter their study ID (auto-filled when arriving from a personalized link).

  1. This week, how helpful were your conversations with the AI support agent? (Not at all helpful / Slightly / Moderately / Very / Extremely helpful; N/A did not use)
  2. Working Alliance Inventory — Short Revised (WAI-SR; Hatcher & Gillaspy, 2006), client version adapted for this study to refer to the AI support agent (12 items; Task, Bond, and Goal subscales, 4 items each; 5-point frequency scale, Seldom / Sometimes / Fairly Often / Very Often / Always; shown unless the participant answered "Not applicable - did not use it this week" on the helpfulness question). Prompt: "Below is a list of statements about experiences people might have with the AI support agent. Thinking about your conversations with the AI support agent this week, choose the category that best describes your own experience. There are no right or wrong answers."
    1. As a result of these sessions I am clearer as to how I might be able to change. (Task)
    2. What I am doing in these sessions gives me new ways of looking at my problem. (Task)
    3. I believe the AI support agent likes me. (Bond)
    4. The AI support agent and I collaborate on setting goals for my sessions. (Goal)
    5. The AI support agent and I respect each other. (Bond)
    6. The AI support agent and I are working towards mutually agreed upon goals. (Goal)
    7. I feel that the AI support agent appreciates me. (Bond)
    8. The AI support agent and I agree on what is important for me to work on. (Goal)
    9. I feel the AI support agent cares about me even when I do things that it does not approve of. (Bond)
    10. I feel that the things I do in these sessions will help me to accomplish the changes that I want. (Task)
    11. The AI support agent and I have established a good understanding of the kind of changes that would be good for me. (Goal)
    12. I believe the way we are working with my problem is correct. (Task)

    Note displayed to participants at the end of the measure: "Working Alliance Inventory – Short Revised (WAI-SR; Hatcher & Gillaspy, 2006), adapted for this study to refer to the AI support agent. Items copyright © Adam Horvath. Reprinted by permission of the Society for Psychotherapy Research © 2026."

  3. How would you rate your overall mood this week? (Very poor / Poor / Fair / Good / Very good / Excellent)
  4. How stressed did you feel this week? (Not at all / Slightly / Moderately / Very / Extremely)
  5. Emotional dependence on the AI support agent (5 items; shown to all participants, including those who did not use the agent this week). Prompt: "Thinking about the past week, how much do you agree or disagree with each statement about the AI support agent? There are no right or wrong answers." (Strongly disagree / Disagree / Neither agree nor disagree / Agree / Strongly agree)
    1. When I can't use the AI support agent (for example, because of work, travel, or the app being closed overnight), I feel an unbearable emptiness.
    2. I honestly believe that if I stopped using the AI support agent, I would not be able to bear it.
    3. Compared with other people who use support apps, I sincerely believe that I need the AI support agent more than they need theirs.
    4. Honestly, I always need the AI support agent available to me.
    5. I think I am emotionally dependent on the AI support agent.

    Source and adaptation: Craving subscale (items 1, 2, 4, 6, 9) of the Affective Dependence Scale, ADS-9 (Sirvent-Ruiz, C. M., Moral-Jiménez, M. V., Herrero, J., Miranda-Roves, M., & Rodríguez Díaz, F. J. (2022). Concept of affective dependence and validation of an affective dependence scale. Psychology Research and Behavior Management, 15, 3875-3888. https://doi.org/10.2147/PRBM.S385807). Published under CC BY-NC 3.0, which permits non-commercial use with attribution. "My partner" is replaced by "the AI support agent", item 4 is reworded to compare with other users of support apps, examples of absence fit to this study are added to item 1, and a past-week frame is added. The same subscale, adapted to a chatbot, was the emotional-dependence measure in Fang et al. (2025, arXiv:2503.17473); their exact wording is unpublished. The chatbot adaptation has not been separately validated; internal consistency will be reported from study data. Scoring: mean of the 5 items (1-5).

  6. Problematic use of the AI support agent (6 items; shown to all participants). Prompt: "How often during the past week have you..." (Very rarely / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Very often)
    1. ...spent a lot of time thinking about the AI support agent or planning when to use it?
    2. ...felt an urge to use the AI support agent more and more?
    3. ...used the AI support agent in order to forget about personal problems?
    4. ...tried to cut down on your use of the AI support agent without success?
    5. ...become restless or troubled if you have been unable to use the AI support agent?
    6. ...used the AI support agent so much that it has had a negative impact on your studies or job?

    Source and adaptation: Bergen Social Media Addiction Scale, BSMAS (Andreassen, C. S., Billieux, J., Griffiths, M. D., Kuss, D. J., Demetrovics, Z., Mazzoni, E., & Pallesen, S. (2016). The relationship between addictive use of social media and video games and symptoms of psychiatric disorders: A large-scale cross-sectional study. Psychology of Addictive Behaviors, 30(2), 252-262. https://doi.org/10.1037/adb0000160). "Social media" is replaced by "the AI support agent" (the approach Hu, Mao & Kim, 2023, Computers in Human Behavior 145:107760, used for conversational AI), "prohibited from using" by "unable to use", "job/studies" by "studies or job", and the time frame "during the last year" by "during the past week". Scoring: sum of the 6 items (6-30). Because of the changed time frame, published cut-offs are used only as a review trigger, not as a classification.

  7. Did anything about your conversations with the AI support agent bother you, upset you, or feel wrong this week? (No / Yes - please describe, free text). Responses describing distress are reviewed by the study team within one business day; participants in need are contacted with support resources. An inline notice with crisis resources is shown when "Yes" is selected.
  8. Did you seek support from a person (friend, family, counselor) for something you also discussed with the AI agent this week? (Yes / No / Prefer not to say)
  9. Anything else you want the research team to know this week? (free text, optional)
