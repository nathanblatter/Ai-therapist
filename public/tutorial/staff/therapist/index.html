How to use the AI Support Agent admin portal as a therapist: the clinical-oversight role. You hold the full clinical data tier — session transcripts (redacted), AI clinical summaries, screener trajectories, and signed notes. Screenshots come from a practice sandbox with entirely synthetic clients; your own account starts in exactly such a sandbox (the amber banner) until you're assigned real participants.

1Your caseload

Your landing page: every client assigned to you, plus Client Invites — one-time links that register a new client directly onto your caseload. Everything else in the portal is scoped to these people; you never see clients who aren't yours.

2Triage — who needs you today

The attention-ranked roster: open crisis flags, open escalations, safety plans, low check-in moods, and overdue practice all surface as chips, and the most urgent client sorts to the top with an attention score. Start here each day; click any row to open the full profile.

3The Participant Profile — what the AI remembers

The clinical heart of the portal. Top strip: PHQ-2/GAD-2 and mood with trend arrows, session count, and risk state. Below: an AI-generated summary (always verify against the record — it says so on the tin), then the session timeline with crisis tags, safety-plan events, per-session topics, moods, and follow-ups. Deeper in the profile: the prep brief ("since you last looked…"), risk timeline chart, session insights, and the notes panel. This is also where you start a progress note from the AI's SOAP draft.

4Work Queue — ack and resolve

Everything that needs a human lands here: crisis flags, inbound escalations, worsening screeners, stale unread messages, inactivity. Ack claims an item; Resolve closes it with a short note that becomes part of the audit trail. Below the queue, set your notification preferences — daily digest timing and whether urgent safety items email you immediately (they should).

5Escalations — hand-offs from caseworkers

Caseworkers raise structured escalations to you ("client disclosed passive ideation during a check-in; requesting a clinical follow-up call"). Acknowledge, work it, and resolve with a disposition — the red nav badge counts what's still open. This is the clinical backstop path the study protocol depends on, so open escalations are your first priority after crisis flags.

6Messages

Secure asynchronous messaging with clients on your caseload, started from a client's profile. Participants are told replies take 1-2 business days, and every participant message is automatically safety-scanned — anything alarming also lands in Crisis Management and your work queue, so the inbox is never the only net.

7Also in your nav

  • Live Monitoring / Sessions / Analytics — watch active sessions in real time (with sideband controls to inject a message or end a session), browse full session history, and see usage analytics.
  • Crisis Management — all crisis events and interventions for your clients; you can clear flags and record wind-downs.
  • Adverse Events — review and sign off IRB adverse-event reports involving your clients.
  • Clinical notes — SOAP-structured, autosaving drafts; signing makes a note immutable (amend-only afterward). Sign only what you've verified.
  • Export / Rate Limits — session data export and rate-limited user list.

Sandbox first: practice every workflow above on your synthetic caseload — nothing there reaches real participants, pagers, or email. Version 2026-09-04.

Questions or something looks wrong? Use the Report-a-problem button in the app — reports go straight to the team.
